## VO script (record this exactly)

Whatever you are trying to do has a length.

Learning something takes the time it takes. Building something takes the time it takes.
Getting fit, saving money, writing the thing.

None of that gets shorter because you waited.

So starting later does not shrink the work. It moves the finish.

That is the problem with keeping it in your head. There is no start date in there.
Nothing to move, and nothing to miss.

What deemyor does is give the thing a start date, in public, that you cannot quietly
move later.

You write down what you are going to do. It gets today's date. It goes into a public
archive and it stays there.

Phase one is live now. It will not remind you and it will not track you. Phase two is
not built yet.

But the date is real, and the date is today.

Deemyor dot com.

## The argument, in order

Every line is a statement, and each one follows from the one before it.

| # | statement | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the work has a fixed length | premise |
| 2 | waiting does not shorten it | follows from 1 |
| 3 | therefore delay moves the finish, not the effort | follows from 2 |
| 4 | a goal in your head has no start date | observation |
| 5 | so there is nothing to move and nothing to miss | follows from 4 |
| 6 | dmyor supplies a dated public start | what it does |
| 7 | it does not remind or track; phase two is unbuilt | what it does not do |
| 8 | the date is real and it is today | the only claim |

Nothing here asks the viewer how they feel, and there is no invented statistic. The
strongest line is **"It moves the finish"**, because it reframes delay as a cost that is
already being paid rather than a decision still to be made.

**"Nothing to move, and nothing to miss" is doing quiet work.** It concedes the appeal of
keeping things vague: an undated goal cannot be failed. That is the honest reason people
do it, stated without judging anyone for it.
