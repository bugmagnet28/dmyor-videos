# Retention playbook (2026-11-14)

How every reel from now on hooks, holds and gets sent. Built from web research (sources at the end) plus our own data (`analytics/reports/2026-11-14-analysis.md`). Where this and `content-kit-v2.md` disagree, this wins.

## What Instagram rewards
1. **Watch time and completion** (time watched vs length). Strongest signal.
2. **Sends per reach** (DM shares). Biggest signal for reaching non-followers.
3. Likes per reach (matters more with existing followers).
New reels get a cold-start test: early watch time decides whether they get pushed further.

## The first 3 seconds (decides most of the result)
- First-3-second hold predicts final views (r about 0.84 in one 120-video test). Target: 70%+ still watching at 3 s.
- **Layer two hooks:** a visual that demands explanation + a verbal/text curiosity gap. Layered hooks held 47% more than single-mechanism hooks.
- On Reels, **relatable pain points** work best ("you know that feeling when..."). Curiosity gaps are the most reliable overall.
- About half of Reels start muted: frame 0 must work silently. Text 6 words max.
- **Silent 3-second test before publishing:** with sound off, can you tell what it's about, who should care, and why to keep watching?
- Never open with a greeting, a logo, a title card, backstory or the series question.

## Our hook shape (from our 6 hits)
**"The thing you do because you're trying hard is exactly what's holding you back."**
- Domain: body or skill people actively work on (gym, running, sleep, studying, an instrument, speaking).
- Frame 0: the viewer's own pattern drawn as a chart/scene, the mistake highlighted, one number.
- Line 1 is the roast or the pain: "1,000 crunches a day. Belly: unchanged."

## Holding them (middle)
- **Open a loop by second 3** ("and it's not what you think"), and every time you close one, open the next (re-hook).
- **Re-hooks** at about 40% and 65% of the reel: "but that's not even the worst part", "here's the part nobody does".
- **Something changes on screen every 2-4 s** (new element, zoom, number count, cut).
- **Put the payoff at about two-thirds**, not at the end. Most people never reach the end.
- One **sendable line** in the middle: "Send this to the friend who does 1,000 crunches."

## Ending
- **Loop:** the last frame matches frame 0 (same chart, now fixed), so the replay looks intentional and counts as rewatch.
- End on a short, easy, personal comment question.
- Quick-hit reels end within 2 s of the payoff. No long brand outros (brand card max 1.5 s, or caption only).

## Tone: roast and sarcasm
- Humour-led short videos get about 2x the engagement of plain informational ones; roasts trigger ego and comments.
- **Roast the habit, never the person.** The narrator is on the viewer's side ("we've all done it"). Audiences punish arrogance fast; a calm, dry voice wins.
- One joke per beat, then real help. The fix must be genuinely useful, or the joke reads as mockery.
- No mean words about bodies, intelligence or money situations.

## Formats in rotation
| Format | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Habit Roast (quick-hit) | 15-25 s | roast line + chart on frame 0 -> twist (mechanism) -> fix -> loop |
| Kabir vs. Kabir | 40-50 s | sarcastic narrator, story, mechanism shown, fix, loop |
| Straight Answer | 30-45 s | calm, chart-led, one mechanism, one fix |
| Small Stories | 35-45 s | two people, one difference, time passes |
| Explainer (illustrated) | 45-65 s | the hit format; concrete scene + number on frame 0 |

The "Why do we stop doing things..." card is OFF inside reels for now (V-EXP-35). "Reason #n" may appear as a small badge and in the caption.

## Checklist (score before render)
- [ ] Frame 0 finished, readable muted, one number, 6 words max
- [ ] Line 1 = roast/pain in under 2 s of speech
- [ ] Loop opened by 3 s
- [ ] Re-hook at ~40% and ~65%
- [ ] Visual change every 2-4 s
- [ ] Payoff at ~2/3
- [ ] One sendable line
- [ ] Last frame mirrors frame 0
- [ ] Comment question
- [ ] Facts true; no invented studies or numbers presented as research
