# Quality bar (every reel must pass before Sanyam sees it)

Owner direction (2026-10-24): post **3-5 reels per day**. Nobody knows us yet, so volume matters, but **quality decides everything**: each reel must be interesting enough to stop the scroll and **useful enough that a viewer can do something differently today**.

## The one-line test
> After watching, could a stranger **do** one specific thing they could not (or would not) do before?
If the answer is "they'd feel motivated", it fails. Motivation is a by-product; the reel must hand over a **tool, a method, a reframe with a concrete next step, or a worked example**.

## Score it (1-5 each, write the scores in the reel's `script.meta.json` as `"quality": {...}`)

| # | Criterion | 5 looks like | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook (first 1.5 s)** | A specific tension or surprising claim the viewer recognises in themselves ("You don't have a motivation problem. You have a size problem.") | Generic ("Want to achieve your goals?"), a logo, an empty frame |
| 2 | **Usefulness** | One concrete method/tool/template the viewer can apply today, with a real example | Vague advice, platitudes, "believe in yourself" |
| 3 | **Specificity and logic** | Numbers, names, real situations that a sceptical adult agrees would work ("Earn $2K/month freelancing: 3 proposals this week") | Abstract nouns with no example, or illogical advice ("get fit: put your shoes by the door") |
| 4 | **Clarity** | One idea, one takeaway; a 12-year-old could repeat it | Two or three ideas competing; jargon |
| 5 | **Retention design** | Something new every 1.5-3 s; a payoff saved for the end (reveal, before/after, the "today" action) | Static stretches, payoff in the first 3 s then nothing, scenes that finish early and sit empty |
| 6 | **Craft** | Clean frames, nothing clipped, text readable on a phone, the style skill's checks pass | Overflow, tiny text, timing drift, dead air |
| 7 | **Voice** | Written for the ear, staged delivery, warm complete ending | Clipped lines, robotic run-on, abrupt stop |
| 8 | **Honest brand fit** | Dmyor appears where it genuinely helps; locked features only as "full features are releasing soon" | Hard sell, fake claims, implying locked features work |

**Ship only if**: total ≥ 30/40 **and** Hook ≥ 4 **and** Usefulness ≥ 4. Otherwise rewrite (most failures are fixed in the script, not the render).

## Owner rules, 2026-10-24 (apply to every style)
1. **No duration cap.** Explain the idea completely: no empty or half-finished explanation, and no padding. Typical result 35-55 s. Every thought must add something new.
2. **It must make sense.** Every example has to be logical and credible to a sceptical adult. Ask: "would this actually help with that goal?" Bad: "Get fit? Put your shoes by the door." Good: "Walk thirty minutes after dinner, three days this week." Use real methods with real numbers. Don't dismiss the big plan as the mistake: the big plan is a middle level of the breakdown. Example: "Get fit" → go to the gym on a 6-day program → take it one day at a time → today is just chest day (bench press 3 x 8...). Never "don't start with a 6-day program".
3. **Brand close always explains the value**, never just "That's Dmyor": "That's what Dmyor is for. You archive your dream today, so you can see it tomorrow, and break it down into small steps you can actually do." then "If you want to try it out, I've put the link in the bio." Show a visual during the value sentence (e.g. the illustrated `dream-to-steps` scene); the brand card only holds the final line.
4. **Mature, classy motion.** Ease-in-out and ease-out only (no bounce, no overshoot, no wobble), small movements (under 40 px), thin consistent strokes, no cartoon effects (sparkles, bobbing "?", shaking). Calm crossfades between scenes.
5. **Palette: Graphite & Sage.** bg `#151719`, ink `#E9E7E2`, mute `#8C8F8A`, accent sage `#9DB8A3`, secondary `#D8CBA8`, warning `#C98B7B`, panel `#1D2023`, line `#33373B`. No orange.
6. **Few, short gaps.** Pauses between thoughts 0.18-0.32 s (say.py defaults); no dead air on screen either: every scene keeps something moving or resolving until the next thought.

## Useful formats (rotate; each must still pass the test)
1. **Method in 3 steps, with a worked example** (goal → milestones → today's action, on a real goal).
2. **The mistake + the fix** ("Your plan only works on good days → write a bad-day version: run 5K → run 1 lap").
3. **Before → after** (a vague goal rewritten into a plan you can start tonight).
4. **One number that reframes** (20 min/day = 121 hours/year; what that buys you), then what to do with it.
5. **A tiny template to save** ("Copy this: When ___ I will ___ for ___ minutes").
6. **Myth vs reality** (motivation first vs action first), with the one action.
7. **Real app walkthrough** (hybrid style: write your vision in 60 seconds).
8. **Comment-to-reel**: break down a goal someone posted, step by step.

## Volume without filler
- Each day: 3-5 reels, **at least 2 different style skills**, **no two reels with the same takeaway** in the same week.
- Space them through the day (≥ 2-3 h apart) so they don't compete for the same test audience; exact times don't matter (owner).
- Batch production: script all of the day's reels first, score them, drop the weakest idea before rendering anything.
- Keep a stock of ≥ 5 approved-but-unposted reels so a bad day never forces a weak post.
